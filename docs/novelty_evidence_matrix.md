# Novelty and Evidence Matrix

## DARA-DT — Divergence-Aware Runtime Assurance for Digital Twins

**Research Domain:** Trustworthy Intelligent Systems  
**Experimental Domain:** Autonomous Logistics Digital Twins  
**Experimental Programme:** EXP-001 to EXP-010  
**Status:** COMPLETE  
**Evidence Position:** Post-Experiment Consolidation  
**Novelty Status:** Bounded by Experimental Evidence

---

# 1. Purpose

This document evaluates the proposed novelty of DARA-DT against the evidence
generated by the completed experimental programme.

Its purpose is not to demonstrate novelty by assertion.

Instead, it separates:

```text
Proposed Research Idea
        ↓
Experimental Question
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Supported Contribution
        ↓
Unsupported Claim
        ↓
Remaining Research Opportunity
```

The central research question is:

> **How can physical–digital divergence be quantified at runtime and used to
> regulate autonomous AI decision-making in dynamic logistics Digital Twins?**

The ten-experiment programme progressively tested increasingly strong versions
of the proposed contribution.

The final interpretation must therefore reflect both:

- positive findings; and
- negative or equivalence findings.

---

# 2. Research Position

The project investigates runtime assurance at the intersection of:

```text
Digital Twins

Artificial Intelligence

Autonomous Decision-Making

Physical–Digital Divergence

Runtime Evidence

Decision Dependencies

Decision Validity

Runtime Assurance

Autonomous Authority
```

The original intuition was that physical–digital divergence could provide a
distinct assurance signal for regulating autonomous AI decisions.

The experimental programme progressively refined that proposition.

The final evidence does not support a broad claim that divergence-aware
assurance universally outperforms strong runtime-contract mechanisms.

Instead, the evidence supports a more precise contribution concerning how
divergence, decision dependencies, validity and evidence quality interact.

# 4. Novelty Evaluation Principle

A feature is not treated as novel merely because it exists in DARA-DT.

For this project, a defensible novelty claim requires evidence that the
proposed contribution is:

1. conceptually distinguishable from relevant prior approaches;
2. operationally defined;
3. experimentally testable;
4. compared against meaningful alternatives;
5. supported by the experimental results; and
6. not eliminated by a stronger comparator.

Accordingly:

```text
Implemented
≠
Novel
```

and:

```text
Different Architecture
≠
Demonstrated Performance Contribution
```

# 5. Evidence Categories

The matrix uses the following classifications.

## Supported

The experimental programme provides direct evidence for the claim within the
tested conditions.

## Partially Supported

Some aspect of the claim is supported, but the broader interpretation is not.

## Not Supported

The experimental evidence does not establish the proposed claim.

## Not Evaluated

The project contains the concept or capability, but the relevant outcome has
not been experimentally evaluated.

## Falsified as a Strong Differentiation Claim

A stronger comparator reproduced the proposed mechanism's primary behaviour,
so the claimed decision-performance differentiation is not supported by the
current evidence.

# 7. Candidate Novelty N1 — Physical–Digital Divergence

## Proposition

The initial proposition was:

> Runtime assurance for autonomous Digital Twins should explicitly consider
> disagreement between the physical system and its Digital Twin.

Represent:

\[
\delta_t = P_t - T_t
\]

where:

- \(P_t\) represents physical state; and
- \(T_t\) represents Digital Twin state.

---

## Evidence

EXP-001 and EXP-002 showed that physical–digital divergence can expose unsafe
situations hidden by stale or incorrect Twin state.

For example, a vehicle can physically fail while its Digital Twin still
represents it as operational.

However, those experiments also demonstrated that irrelevant divergence
should not automatically trigger intervention.

EXP-004 further demonstrated that even decision-relevant divergence does not
necessarily invalidate a decision.

---

## Status

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

---

## Defensible Claim

> Physical–digital divergence is a useful runtime signal, but divergence
> existence alone is insufficient for regulating autonomous authority.

---

## Claim to Avoid

> Physical–digital divergence alone provides a sufficient runtime-assurance
> criterion.

The experiments contradict that stronger interpretation.

---

# 8. Candidate Novelty N2 — Decision-Relevant Divergence

## Proposition

Rather than evaluate all detected divergence globally, DARA-DT conditions
divergence on the dependencies of the pending decision.

For decision \(d_t\):

\[
D_t^{rel}(d_t)
=
Dep(d_t) \cap D_t
\]

---

## Evidence

EXP-001–EXP-003 demonstrated that:

```text
irrelevant divergence
```

and:

```text
decision-relevant divergence
```

can require different assurance responses.

EXP-002 additionally varied the quantity of irrelevant divergence, showing
that mismatch count alone was not a sufficient decision criterion.

---

## Status

**SUPPORTED AS A USEFUL ABSTRACTION**

---

## Limitation

EXP-004 demonstrated:

\[
Decision\ Relevance
\neq
Decision\ Invalidity
\]

Relevant divergence may still be too small or operationally insignificant to
invalidate the pending decision.

---

## Defensible Claim

> Conditioning divergence on decision dependencies provides more meaningful
> runtime context than treating all Digital Twin mismatch globally.

---

## Claim to Avoid

> Every decision-relevant divergence requires intervention.

This is contradicted by EXP-004.

---

# 9. Candidate Novelty N3 — Divergence Severity

## Proposition

The magnitude of physical–digital divergence may help determine whether a
decision remains valid.

---

## Evidence

EXP-004 varied capacity divergence around a known validity boundary.

The experiment demonstrated that:

- global divergence over-intervened;
- decision relevance also over-intervened;
- no assurance missed invalid cases; and
- a fixed magnitude threshold aligned with the synthetic boundary.

---

## Status

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

---

## Limitation

A fixed magnitude threshold aligned with the controlled synthetic boundary but
does not constitute a general decision-validity mechanism.

Different decisions may have different:

```text
dependencies

operational margins

constraints

consequences

validity boundaries
```

---

## Defensible Claim

> Divergence magnitude can matter, but its assurance meaning depends on the
> pending decision and its validity constraints.

---

# 10. Candidate Novelty N4 — Decision Impact / Validity

## Proposition

Runtime assurance should ask whether divergence changes the validity of the
pending autonomous decision.

This moves beyond:

```text
Is the variable divergent?
```

and:

```text
Does the decision depend on the variable?
```

toward:

```text
Does the observed condition invalidate
the decision that is about to execute?
```

---

## Evidence

EXP-005 produced:

```text
Decision Impact

Accuracy = 0.867
Precision = 0.750
Recall = 1.000
Autonomy = 0.467
```

with:

```text
TI = 6
FI = 2
MI = 0
CNI = 7
```

This improved selectivity relative to global divergence and relevance-only
reasoning in the controlled matrix.

---

## Status

**SUPPORTED**

---

## Important Qualification

EXP-007 later demonstrated:

```text
Decision Impact
=
Runtime Contract
```

across its reliable-evidence conditions.

Therefore decision-impact reasoning is supported as useful, but it was not
demonstrated to provide a unique advantage over runtime contracts.

---

# 11. Candidate Novelty N5 — Separation of Truth, Twin and Evidence

## Proposition

Runtime assurance should explicitly distinguish:

```text
Physical Ground Truth
        ≠
Digital Twin State
        ≠
Runtime Evidence
```

---

## Evidence

EXP-006 introduced imperfect runtime evidence and demonstrated that assurance
behaviour changes when observations are incomplete or unreliable.

This separation was retained throughout EXP-007–EXP-010.

Physical ground truth remained evaluator-only.

Runtime policies acted using the information available at runtime.

---

## Status

**SUPPORTED**

---

## Defensible Claim

> Runtime-assurance evaluation should distinguish physical truth, Digital Twin
> representation and runtime evidence rather than treating them as the same
> information source.

This is an important methodological contribution of the experimental
framework.

# 12. Candidate Novelty N6 — Evidence-Aware Assurance

## Proposition

Runtime authority should depend not only on the apparent decision state but
also on the reliability of evidence supporting that state.

Evidence conditions include:

```text
AVAILABLE

STALE

MISSING

CONFLICTING
```

---

## Evidence

EXP-006 demonstrated that imperfect evidence changes assurance performance.

EXP-008 and EXP-009 extended this analysis using runtime-contract comparators.

---

## Status

**SUPPORTED AS AN IMPORTANT MODELLING DIMENSION**

---

## Limitation

Evidence awareness did not itself establish DARA-DT superiority.

Strong uncertainty-aware runtime contracts remained competitive and, in later
experiments, equivalent on primary binary outcomes.

# 13. Candidate Novelty N7 — Decision-Conditioned Evidence Uncertainty

## Proposition

Evidence uncertainty should be interpreted relative to the dependencies of the
pending decision.

Define:

\[
U_t^{rel}(d_t)
=
U_t \cap Dep(d_t)
\]

and:

\[
U_t^{irr}(d_t)
=
U_t \setminus Dep(d_t)
\]

---

## Evidence

EXP-009 evaluated 42 conditions across:

```text
capacity

status

location / availability
```

and:

```text
AVAILABLE

STALE

MISSING

CONFLICTING
```

evidence states.

Global uncertainty achieved:

```text
Accuracy = 0.500
Recall = 0.857
Autonomy = 0.143
```

DARA-DT achieved:

```text
Accuracy = 0.786
Recall = 1.000
Autonomy = 0.286
```

However, the uncertainty-aware runtime contract achieved the same primary
binary metrics as DARA-DT.

---

## Status

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

---

## Defensible Claim

> Decision conditioning can reduce the limitations of global uncertainty
> handling by distinguishing uncertainty relevant to the pending decision.

---

## Claim to Avoid

> Decision-conditioned DARA-DT outperforms strong uncertainty-aware runtime
> contracts.

EXP-009 does not support that claim.

```text

other entities

shared resources

upstream assumptions
```

A local contract may therefore be insufficient.

```text
MI  = 0

MI Rate = 0.000
Autonomy = 0.267
```

---

## Status

**SUPPORTED WITHIN THE EXP-010 MATRIX**

---

## Defensible Claim

> Some multi-stage autonomous logistics decisions require assurance reasoning
> across decision, entity and shared-resource dependencies rather than only
> immediate local state.

# 15. Candidate Novelty N9 — Explicit Divergence Propagation

## Proposition

Physical–digital divergence can originate upstream and propagate through a
multi-stage decision chain.

Conceptually:

```text
Physical–Digital Divergence
        ↓
Upstream Decision
        ↓
Affected Shared Dependency
        ↓
Downstream Decision
        ↓
Decision Validity
```

---

## Evidence

EXP-010 contained:

```text
24 divergent conditions

12 propagating conditions

12 non-propagating divergent conditions

6 compound propagation conditions
```

The framework distinguished upstream divergence that propagated from upstream
divergence that did not affect the pending decision.

Kill Test I was not triggered.

---

## Status

**SUPPORTED AS A REPRESENTATIONAL CAPABILITY**

---

## Important Qualification

The experiment demonstrates that propagation can be modelled.

It does not demonstrate that explicit propagation provenance improves binary
assurance performance over a strong composed runtime contract.

# 35. Implications for the PhD Proposal

The PhD proposal should not be framed around proving that DARA-DT is already
superior.

A stronger academic framing is:

> Existing experiments indicate that decision-sensitive and cross-dependency
> reasoning are important for runtime assurance in dynamic logistics Digital
> Twins, while also revealing equivalence between divergence-aware mechanisms
> and strong composed runtime contracts on decision-level outcomes. This
> creates a sharper research problem concerning when physical–digital
> divergence provenance contributes information beyond operational constraint
> satisfaction, particularly for explanation, diagnosis, recovery and
> assurance evidence under dynamic uncertainty.

This framing turns the negative result into a research question rather than
hiding it.
