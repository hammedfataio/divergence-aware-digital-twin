# DARA-DT — Experimental Results

**Project:** Divergence-Aware Runtime Assurance for AI-Driven Digital Twins  
**Framework:** DARA-DT  
**Research Stage:** Controlled proof-of-concept evaluation  
**Status:** EXP-001 to EXP-009 completed

---

## 1. Purpose

This document consolidates the experimental evidence generated by the
DARA-DT research prototype.

The experiments investigate the central research question:

> **How can physical–digital divergence be quantified at runtime and used to
> regulate autonomous AI decision-making in dynamic logistics Digital Twins?**

The experimental programme is cumulative and falsification-oriented.

Rather than assuming that DARA-DT is superior to simpler assurance
mechanisms, each experiment tests progressively stronger explanations of when
physical–digital divergence should affect autonomous authority.

The current conceptual progression is:

```text
Physical–Digital Divergence
        ↓
Decision Relevance
        ↓
Decision Impact / Validity
        ↓
Evidence Reliability
        ↓
Decision Risk
        ↓
Runtime Assurance
        ↓
Autonomous Authority
```

The experiments also test whether this additional reasoning provides useful
assurance information beyond simpler alternatives such as:

- no assurance;
- global divergence monitoring;
- fixed thresholds;
- entity filtering;
- decision relevance alone;
- direct feasibility checks;
- runtime contracts;
- uncertainty-aware runtime contracts.

Negative results are retained as research evidence.

---

# 2. Evaluation Principles

The experimental programme follows several recurring principles.

## 2.1 Independent Physical Ground Truth

Runtime policies do not determine whether their own decisions are correct.

Physical decision validity is evaluated independently after the assurance
decision.

This separates:

```text
Physical Ground Truth
        ≠
Digital Twin State
        ≠
Runtime Evidence
        ≠
Assurance Decision
```

---

## 2.2 Controlled Divergence Injection

Physical–digital divergence is introduced deliberately so that the experiments
retain knowledge of:

- what changed;
- when it changed;
- which state variable changed;
- whether the changed variable is relevant to the pending decision;
- whether the divergence changes physical decision validity.

This enables controlled evaluation of assurance behaviour.

---

## 2.3 Safety and Autonomy

The experiments do not treat intervention frequency as a sufficient measure of
assurance quality.

A policy that intervenes on everything may avoid unsafe autonomous execution
but destroy useful autonomy.

Likewise, a policy that always allows execution preserves autonomy but may
permit invalid decisions.

The central trade-off is therefore:

\[
Safety
\leftrightarrow
Autonomy\ Availability
\]

---

## 2.4 Outcome Categories

Where applicable, assurance decisions are classified as:

- **TI — True Intervention**
- **FI — False Intervention**
- **MI — Missed Intervention**
- **CNI — Correct Non-Intervention**

These categories distinguish correct safety intervention from unnecessary
restriction and unsafe permissiveness.

---

# 3. Experimental Progression

The completed programme currently contains nine experiments.

| Experiment | Primary Question |
|---|---|
| EXP-001 | Can decision relevance distinguish relevant from irrelevant divergence? |
| EXP-002 | Does relevance remain useful as divergence count changes? |
| EXP-003 | How should decision relevance be represented formally? |
| EXP-004 | Is decision relevance alone sufficient for intervention? |
| EXP-005 | Does decision-specific impact improve intervention quality? |
| EXP-006 | What happens when runtime evidence is imperfect? |
| EXP-007 | Does impact reasoning generalise across dependency types? |
| EXP-008 | Does DARA-DT add value beyond runtime contracts under imperfect evidence? |
| EXP-009 | Does decision relevance to evidence uncertainty improve the safety–autonomy trade-off? |

The experiments should be interpreted as one cumulative research programme
rather than nine independent demonstrations.

---

# 4. EXP-001 — B-vs-C Pilot

## 4.1 Objective

EXP-001 tested whether the assurance mechanism could distinguish between:

- physical–digital divergence unrelated to the current decision; and
- physical–digital divergence affecting a dependency of the current decision.

The pilot deliberately held the detected divergence count equal across the
two principal conditions.

This isolated **decision relevance** rather than divergence quantity.

---

## 4.2 Results

| Condition | No Assurance | Global Divergence | DARA-DT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision-irrelevant divergence | Correct non-intervention | False intervention | Correct non-intervention |
| Decision-relevant divergence | Missed intervention | True intervention | True intervention |

---

## 4.3 Finding

The pilot demonstrated that divergence existence alone is too coarse.

When divergence affected unrelated state, global divergence assurance
intervened unnecessarily.

When divergence affected the selected decision, unrestricted autonomy allowed
an invalid decision while both global divergence assurance and DARA-DT
intervened.

The bounded conclusion was:

> **Decision relevance can distinguish divergence that affects a pending
> decision from divergence that does not.**

This was preliminary evidence only.

It did not establish that relevance alone is sufficient for assurance.

---

# 5. EXP-002 — Controlled Policy Comparison

## 5.1 Objective

EXP-002 expanded the pilot into a seven-condition controlled matrix.

Four conditions contained decision-irrelevant divergence with divergence
counts of:

```text
1
5
10
25
```

Three conditions introduced divergence affecting the selected vehicle's:

- operational status;
- capacity;
- availability.

The experiment compared:

- No Assurance;
- Global Divergence Assurance;
- Decision-Relevant Divergence Assurance.

---

## 5.2 Finding

The experiment strengthened the distinction between:

\[
Amount\ of\ Divergence
\]

and:

\[
Decision\ Relevance
\]

Increasing the amount of unrelated divergence did not make the current
decision physically invalid.

Conversely, divergence affecting a required decision dependency could require
intervention even when the overall system did not contain large amounts of
mismatch.

The experiment therefore motivated explicit dependency mapping rather than
global divergence-count reasoning.

---

# 6. EXP-003 — Decision-Relevance Formalisation

## 6.1 Objective

EXP-003 formalised the relationship between detected divergence and the
dependencies of an AI-generated decision.

For a decision \(d_t\):

\[
Dep(d_t)
\]

represents the dependencies required by that decision.

Let:

\[
D_t
\]

represent detected physical–digital divergence.

Decision-relevant divergence is:

\[
D_t^{rel}(d_t)=Dep(d_t)\cap D_t
\]

---

## 6.2 Finding

EXP-003 established decision relevance as an explicit computational stage.

The resulting assurance pipeline became:

```text
Detected Divergence
        ↓
Decision Dependencies
        ↓
Decision-Relevant Divergence
```

However, this raised a more difficult question:

> If divergence affects a required dependency, does that necessarily mean the
> proposed decision is unsafe?

EXP-004 tested that question directly.

---

# 7. EXP-004 — Divergence Severity and Validity

## 7.1 Objective

EXP-004 varied physical vehicle capacity while the Digital Twin retained a
stale capacity value.

The experiment tested whether decision relevance alone could distinguish
between:

```text
Relevant divergence + valid decision
```

and:

```text
Relevant divergence + invalid decision
```

---

## 7.2 Central Result

The experiment showed that relevance alone over-intervened.

For example:

```text
Twin capacity     = 10
Physical capacity = 9
Order demand      = 5
```

The divergence is decision relevant because vehicle capacity is required by
the assignment decision.

However:

```text
9 >= 5
```

so the assignment remains physically feasible.

The central finding was therefore:

> **Decision relevance is not equivalent to decision invalidity.**

---

## 7.3 Research Consequence

This was an important negative result.

It prevented the research from treating every relevant mismatch as sufficient
reason to remove autonomous authority.

The conceptual chain therefore expanded from:

```text
Divergence
    ↓
Relevance
```

to:

```text
Divergence
    ↓
Relevance
    ↓
Decision Impact / Validity
```

---

EXP-005 introduced explicit decision-impact reasoning and evaluated five
policies across 15 controlled capacity conditions.

The experiment did not establish a perfect assurance mechanism.

# 9. EXP-006 — Imperfect Evidence

## 9.1 Objective

EXP-006 challenged the assumption that assurance has direct access to reliable
physical state.

The experiment separated:

\[
Physical\ Ground\ Truth
\]

from:

\[
Runtime\ Evidence
\]

and evaluated assurance when evidence could be imperfect.

---

## 9.2 Key Results

### Decision Relevance

```text
TI  = 9
FI  = 3
MI  = 0
CNI = 0
```

```text
Accuracy  = 0.750
Recall    = 1.000
Autonomy  = 0.000
```

### Deterministic Decision Impact

```text
TI  = 9
FI  = 0
MI  = 0
CNI = 3
```

```text
Accuracy  = 1.000
Recall    = 1.000
Autonomy  = 0.250
```

### Evidence-Aware Decision Impact

```text
TI  = 8
FI  = 1
MI  = 1
CNI = 2
```

```text
Accuracy  = 0.833
Precision = 0.889
Recall    = 0.889
Autonomy  = 0.250
```

---

## 9.3 Finding

The deterministic impact mechanism performed perfectly within the matrix, but
it received reliable evidence and therefore represented an idealised baseline.

The evidence-aware mechanism did not reproduce this result.

It produced:

- one false intervention;
- one missed intervention.

This demonstrated that:

> **Correct impact logic does not guarantee correct assurance when the
> evidence supporting that logic is itself unreliable.**

This result introduced evidence reliability as a first-class component of the
research problem.

The conceptual chain became:

```text
Divergence
    ↓
Decision Relevance
    ↓
Decision Impact
    ↓
Evidence Reliability
    ↓
Runtime Assurance
```

# 10. EXP-007 — Cross-Dependency Generalisation

## 10.1 Objective

EXP-007 tested whether the earlier findings were confined to vehicle capacity.

The experiment introduced three dependency families:

- capacity;
- operational status;
- location / availability.

It also introduced a strong comparator:

> **Runtime Contract**

---

## 10.2 Aggregate Results

| Policy | Correct | TI | FI | MI | CNI | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No Assurance | 9/12 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 9 | 75% |
| Global Divergence | 6/12 | 3 | 6 | 0 | 3 | 50% |
| Decision Relevance | 9/12 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 6 | 75% |
| Decision Impact | 12/12 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 9 | 100% |
| Runtime Contract | 12/12 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 9 | 100% |

---

## 10.3 Positive Finding

Decision-impact reasoning reproduced its controlled behaviour across:

- capacity;
- status;
- location / availability.

This provided bounded evidence that the mechanism was not capacity-specific.

---

## 10.4 Falsification Finding

The Runtime Contract also achieved:

\[
12/12
\]

correct outcomes.

Therefore:

\[
DecisionImpact = RuntimeContract
\]

at the binary outcome level in EXP-007.

This was a major result.

It showed that demonstrating good DARA-DT performance was not sufficient.

The research now needed to answer:

> **What assurance information does DARA-DT provide that cannot already be
> represented by a simpler runtime contract?**

This question became central to subsequent experiments.

# 12. EXP-009 — Decision-Relevant Evidence Uncertainty

\[

# 18. Cumulative Evidence

The nine experiments produce the following cumulative evidence chain.

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| EXP-001 | Decision relevance can distinguish relevant from irrelevant divergence |
| EXP-002 | Divergence quantity alone is insufficient |
| EXP-003 | Decision relevance can be represented through explicit dependency matching |
| EXP-004 | Relevance does not imply decision invalidity |
| EXP-005 | Decision-impact reasoning reduces unnecessary intervention in the controlled capacity matrix |
| EXP-006 | Assurance quality depends on runtime evidence reliability |
| EXP-007 | Impact reasoning generalises across three dependency families, but runtime-contract equivalence appears |
| EXP-008 | Runtime-contract equivalence persists under imperfect evidence |
| EXP-009 | Decision-sensitive uncertainty handling outperforms global uncertainty behaviour, but DARA-DT remains equivalent to the uncertainty-aware runtime contract on primary binary outcomes |

The research progression can therefore be represented as:

```text
Divergence exists
        ↓
Does it affect this decision?
        ↓
Does it change the decision's validity?
        ↓
Can the runtime evidence supporting that judgement be trusted?
        ↓
Should autonomous authority be changed?
        ↓
Can a simpler runtime contract make the same assurance decision?
```

The final question is now central.

# 19. What the Evidence Currently Supports

The completed experiments provide controlled evidence for several bounded
claims.

## 19.1 Global Divergence Is Too Coarse

The presence or quantity of physical–digital mismatch alone is insufficient
for selective intervention.

---

## 19.2 Decision Relevance Is Useful

Mapping divergence to the dependencies of a specific decision can distinguish
potentially relevant mismatch from unrelated system mismatch.

---

## 19.3 Relevance Is Not Enough

A divergence can affect a decision dependency without invalidating the
decision.

Therefore:

\[
Relevant \not\Rightarrow Invalid
\]

---

## 19.4 Decision Impact Matters

The consequence of divergence for physical decision feasibility provides
information beyond relevance alone.

---

## 19.5 Evidence Reliability Matters

Correct assurance reasoning can fail when its runtime evidence is stale,
missing, or conflicting.

Therefore assurance cannot assume that observed runtime state equals physical
ground truth.

---

## 19.6 Decision-Sensitive Uncertainty Can Reduce Global Conservatism

EXP-009 demonstrates that globally uncertain evidence can cause unnecessary
intervention when the uncertainty is unrelated to the pending decision.

Decision-sensitive assurance can avoid some of this conservatism.

---

## 19.7 Runtime Contracts Are a Strong Comparator

EXP-007, EXP-008, and EXP-009 repeatedly show that a strong runtime contract
can reproduce the primary binary outcomes of the current DARA-DT mechanism
within the tested decision structures.

This is now a central research finding.

---

# 20. What the Evidence Does Not Support

The current experiments do **not** establish that:

- DARA-DT generally outperforms runtime contracts;
- DARA-DT provides higher aggregate autonomy than the strongest current
  comparator;
- DARA-DT is superior across real logistics systems;
- the current results generalise beyond the controlled experimental matrices;
- decision relevance alone is sufficient for safe assurance;
- Digital Twin divergence automatically implies unsafe AI behaviour;
- all non-ALLOW authority states should have the same operational meaning;
- the current framework constitutes confirmed research novelty.

In particular, the following claim is **not supported**:

> DARA-DT outperforms uncertainty-aware runtime contracts.

The evidence requires a narrower interpretation.

---

# 21. Current Contribution Statement

The evidence currently supports the following bounded contribution statement:

> **DARA-DT provides an experimental framework for studying how
> physical–digital divergence, decision dependencies, decision validity, and
> runtime evidence quality interact in runtime assurance for AI-driven
> logistics Digital Twins. Controlled experiments demonstrate the value of
> decision-sensitive assurance relative to global divergence or uncertainty
> monitoring, while also showing that strong uncertainty-aware runtime
> contracts can reproduce the same binary safety–autonomy trade-off for the
> decision structures evaluated so far.**

This is intentionally not a superiority claim.

---

# 22. Current Novelty Question

The completed experiments sharpen the novelty question to:

> **Under what runtime decision structures, if any, does explicit
> decision-conditioned physical–digital divergence provide assurance
> information that cannot be represented adequately by ordinary runtime
> contracts?**

EXP-007 first exposed this problem.

EXP-008 reproduced it under imperfect evidence.

EXP-009 reproduced it after explicitly separating decision-relevant and
decision-irrelevant uncertainty.

Therefore runtime-contract equivalence can no longer be treated as an
incidental result.

It is now an empirical challenge to the candidate contribution.

---

# 23. Why the Negative Result Matters

The purpose of the research prototype is not to engineer conditions under
which DARA-DT always wins.

If a simpler mechanism explains the same behaviour, that mechanism must be
treated as a serious comparator.

The repeated runtime-contract equivalence improves the scientific value of the
programme because it prevents an unsupported novelty claim.

The evidence currently suggests that the tested decisions may be sufficiently
local and explicit to be represented by runtime contracts.

The next experiment should therefore test a genuinely different decision
structure rather than merely increase the number of similar conditions.

---

# 24. Remaining Evaluation Limitation

EXP-009 exposes an important limitation of the current outcome model.

The binary evaluator treats:

```text
RESTRICT
DEFER
FALLBACK
```

as intervention.

However, these authority states may have different operational consequences.

For example:

```text
RESTRICT
    ↓
execute under constrained authority

DEFER
    ↓
wait for better evidence

FALLBACK
    ↓
transfer control to another mechanism
```

EXP-009 already shows different authority-state distributions between DARA-DT
and the runtime contract even though their binary outcomes are identical.

The present experiments cannot determine whether those semantic differences
have operational value.

This should be addressed explicitly before making authority-level claims.

---

# 25. Candidate Next Experimental Direction

Any subsequent experiment should be separately pre-registered.

It should not be designed merely to obtain a favourable DARA-DT result.

A scientifically justified next experiment would test whether
runtime-contract equivalence persists under richer decision structures such
as:

- compound dependencies across multiple entities;
- interacting physical–digital divergences;
- cascading autonomous decisions;
- temporal dependency chains;
- divergence propagation from one decision to another;
- operational consequences of RESTRICT vs DEFER vs FALLBACK.

A possible next research question is:

> **Does runtime-contract equivalence persist when autonomous logistics
> decisions depend on interacting, cross-entity, or temporally propagating
> physical–digital state dependencies?**

The experiment should contain explicit falsification criteria.

If runtime-contract equivalence persists, the DARA-DT contribution must be
narrowed further.

If equivalence breaks, the mechanism responsible for that difference must be
isolated rather than attributed automatically to the complete framework.

# 26. Threats to Validity

The current evidence remains subject to several limitations.

## 26.1 Controlled Synthetic Conditions

The experiments use deliberately constructed scenarios.

They are useful for mechanism isolation but do not represent the full
variability of operational logistics systems.

---

## 26.2 Simplified Decision Structures

The current vehicle-assignment decisions rely primarily on explicit and local
state dependencies.

This may favour direct runtime-contract representation.

---

## 26.3 Limited Operational Metrics

Most current experiments evaluate immediate decision validity and assurance
outcomes.

They do not yet fully evaluate downstream logistics consequences such as:

- route cost;
- lateness;
- service rate;
- utilisation;
- recovery time;
- cascading disruption.

---

## 26.4 Binary Intervention Evaluation

The current TI/FI/MI/CNI evaluator collapses multiple authority states into a
binary intervention interpretation.

This limits conclusions about adaptive authority.

---

## 26.5 Evidence Models Are Controlled

Stale, missing, and conflicting evidence are deliberately injected.

Real sensor, communication, and Digital Twin evidence may contain more complex
error processes.

---

## 26.6 External Generalisation Is Not Established

The results should not be interpreted as performance estimates for real-world
autonomous logistics systems.

They establish behaviour only within the implemented controlled research
prototype.

# 29. Overall Conclusion

EXP-001 through EXP-009 establish a cumulative controlled investigation of
runtime assurance for AI-driven logistics Digital Twins.

The programme began with a simple observation:

> Not every physical–digital mismatch matters to every AI decision.

It then demonstrated that:

1. decision relevance can filter unrelated divergence;
2. relevance alone does not determine decision invalidity;
3. decision-impact reasoning can reduce unnecessary intervention;
4. imperfect runtime evidence changes assurance reliability;
5. the behaviour generalises across capacity, status, and
   location / availability within controlled matrices;
6. global uncertainty monitoring can be unnecessarily conservative;
7. simple entity filtering can preserve autonomy but may miss unsafe
   decisions;
8. strong uncertainty-aware runtime contracts can reproduce the primary
   binary performance of DARA-DT in the decision structures tested so far.

The most important result after EXP-009 is therefore not a claim that DARA-DT
has already demonstrated superiority.

It is the sharper research question created by repeated falsification:

> **When does explicit decision-conditioned physical–digital divergence add
> assurance value beyond a strong runtime contract?**

That question now defines the next stage of the DARA-DT experimental
programme.
